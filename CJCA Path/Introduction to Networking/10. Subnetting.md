# Subnetting

**1. Submit the decimal representation of the subnet mask from the following CIDR: 10.200.20.0/27**

Ans: 255.255.255.224
Explanation:

`255.255.255.255` to binary = `11111111.11111111.11111111.11111111`

There are 32 bit after convert the CIDR from decimal to binary, we need 27 bits, count from the beginning, we will get:

`11111111.11111111.11111111.11100000`

convert it to decimal by refering the table below:

| 128 | 64 | 32 | 16 | 8 | 4 | 2 | 1 |
|:---:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
|  1  |  1 |  1 |  0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |


128 + 64 + 32 = 255.255.255.224

**2. Submit the broadcast address of the following CIDR: 10.200.20.0/27**

Ans: 10.200.20.31

Explanation:

We know that the CIDR is /27, which means:
- 27 bits are allocated for the network portion.
- The remaining 5 bits are allocated for the host portion.

To calculate the broadcast address, we set all host bits to 1.

Refer to the table below:

| 16 | 8 | 4 | 2 | 1 |
|:--:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
|  1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |

16 + 8 + 4 + 2 + 1 = 31

Therefore, the broadcast address is:

10.200.20.31

**3. Split the network 10.200.20.0/27 into 4 subnets and submit the network address of the 3rd subnet as the answer.**

Ans: 10.200.20.16

Explanation:

Calculate new CIDR:\
4 subnets needed:\
2^2 = 4\
We borrow 2 bits from the host portion, so:\
27 + 2 = 29 (new CIDR)

Calculate the subnet mask:\
/29 = 

| 128 | 64 | 32 | 16 | 8 | 4 | 2 | 1 |
|:---:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
|  1  |  1 |  1 |  1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

128 + 64 + 32 + 16 + 8 = 248

Final Subnet = 255.255.255.248

Increment of the subnet: 256 - 248 = 8

Identify 4 subnets:\
Start from 10.200.20.0

| **Subnet** | **Network Address** | **Broadcast Address** |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **1st** | ```10.200.20.0/29``` | ```10.200.20.7``` |
| **2nd** | ```10.200.20.8/29``` | ```10.200.20.15``` |
| **3rd** | ```10.200.20.16/29``` | ```10.200.20.23``` |
| **4th** | ```10.200.20.24/29``` | ```10.200.20.31``` |

Thus, 3rd subnet's network address = 10.200.20.16
